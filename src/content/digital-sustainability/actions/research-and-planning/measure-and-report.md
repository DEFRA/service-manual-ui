**Applies to:** services and other projects from alpha onwards, on and off CDP, including internal tools and infrastructure projects.

**Lead role families:** Product and delivery

## (i) Model the environmental impact of each option

At the start of a project you may be looking at several approaches or solutions. In alpha, teams prototype and test different ideas to decide which to build in beta.

Model the environmental impact of each option, so that sustainability feeds into the choice.

Where it matters, include systemic effects and changes in behaviour. One example is the rebound effect: when technology makes something more efficient, people may use it more, so the total impact can go up.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, modelling the impact of different options brings co-benefits in terms of:

- cost: models of energy, hardware and data use often point to the cheaper option as well
- delivery time: ruling out high-impact options in alpha avoids rework in beta
- compliance: models give evidence for the business case and the service's sustainability statement

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- accuracy: early models rely on estimates and proxies, so results can be uncertain
- effort: modelling takes time and skills that alpha teams may not have
- scope: systemic effects, such as rebound, are hard to model and may be left out

</div>
</details>

<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Further reading</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

</details>

## (ii) Choose the right performance metrics

Measuring the environmental impact of a service or project as it develops shows you where to improve. To do that, you need the right metrics.

Metrics can measure or estimate resource use or pollution directly. Where you cannot measure directly, use a proxy, such as page weight.

Read [measuring the sustainability of digital services](/digital-sustainability/measure) for more on choosing metrics.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, choosing the right metrics brings co-benefits in terms of:

- service performance: proxy metrics such as page weight or compute time also track speed and efficiency
- cost: resource metrics often move in line with hosting and storage costs
- compliance: agreed metrics make it easier to report against the Greening Government Commitments and Defra's objectives

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- proxy risk: a proxy may not track real environmental impact, so improving it may not cut emissions
- effort: collecting new metrics may need extra tooling, which uses resources of its own

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Further reading</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

- [How to set performance metrics for your service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/how-to-set-performance-metrics-for-your-service) (GOV.UK Service Manual): setting metrics based on what your service is for and what users need
- [Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) specification](https://sci.greensoftware.foundation/) (Green Software Foundation): a method for calculating software emissions per unit, such as per user or per transaction
- [Web Sustainability Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/) (W3C): guideline 3.1 covers setting goals based on performance and energy impact

</div>
</details>

## (iii) Set a baseline for measurement

To judge whether a service meets its environmental goals, you need something to measure against. This is called a baseline.

Most services are "brownfield": they replace an existing process of some kind rather than starting from nothing. The existing service can be your baseline. As the new service develops, you can also take a snapshot of it at one point in time to use as a baseline.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, setting a baseline brings co-benefits in terms of:

- cost: a baseline makes savings from the new service visible and easier to report
- service performance: the same baseline can show whether the new service is faster or simpler than the old one
- data quality: setting a baseline exposes gaps in current data early, while there is time to fill them

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- data gaps: data on the current process, especially paper or offline steps, may be missing or estimated
- comparability: changes in scope or user numbers can make before and after comparisons misleading

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Further reading</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

- [Why bother to baseline](https://sustainableict.blog.gov.uk/2025/07/01/why-bother-to-baseline/) (Sustainable ICT blog)

</div>
</details>

## (iv) Publish and share measurement approaches

Share how your project measures progress against environmental goals. Other teams can learn from it, and it builds a culture of openness.

Record your approach and metrics in your [digital sustainability statement](/digital-sustainability/service-standard/process). Where you can, share it more widely too, for example in a blog post.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, publishing measurement approaches brings co-benefits in terms of:

- delivery time: other teams can reuse a published method rather than design their own
- data quality: others can spot errors and gaps in a published method
- compliance: a published approach supports the service's sustainability statement and cross-government reporting

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- misreading: figures based on estimates may be misread or used to compare services unfairly
- security: some details, such as hosting set-up or supplier data, may need removing before you publish

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
