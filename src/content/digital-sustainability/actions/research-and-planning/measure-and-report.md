## (i) Model the environmental impacts of different solutions under consideration

At the start of projects, a range of approaches or solutions may be under consideration.

For services following the Agile lifecycle, the Alpha phase specifically focuses on prototyping and testing different ideas in order to determine which to build in the Beta phase.

Modelling the environmental impacts of different solutions under consideration enables teams to better understand and factor sustainability into decision making about the best options to choose.

Where appropriate, modelling should include consideration of the systemic effects and impacts on behaviours, such as potential rebound effects from the use of technology.

## (ii) Choose appropriate performance metrics

Measuring the environmental impacts of a service or project as it develops helps teams identify areas for improvement. To do so, we need to choose the right metrics.

Metrics can focus on direct measurement or estimation of resource use or pollution, as well as proxy measures where appropriate.

See the [Measure the sustainability of digital services](/digital-sustainability/measure) page for more details on choosing metrics.

---

## (iii) Set a baseline for measurement

Judging the effectiveness of a service in delivering on environmental goals requires that we have something to measure against, a so-called ‘baseline’.

In most cases, a service will be ‘brownfield’, and will replace an existing process in some form or other. This existing (as-is) service can form the baseline against which we measure. As the new solution develops, we can also take a snapshot of it at a moment in time to form a baseline.

## (iv) Publish and share measurement approaches

Sharing how projects approach measuring progress against environmental goals allows us to learn from one another’s efforts and fosters a culture of transparency.

Publish and share your projects’s approach to sustainability measurement where possible.
