### (i) Set page weight budgets

A page weight budget is an upper limit of the size of a webpage, measured in bytes.

Larger webpages mean more data transfer, and with it an increase in energy consumption.

A page budget can bring sustainability benefits as it helps:

- Encourage designing for lightweight pages by making page weight a constraint that designers, developers and content teams consider throughout delivery
- Prevent page weights creeping upwards as new features, media and technologies are added

## (ii) Design for text-based journeys

Design webpages and user journeys as 'text-based' first. This means that all core content and tasks can be understood and completed without the need for images, videos or other media to communicate the steps involved.

Designing for text-based journeys also supports the the principle of 'progressive enhancement'.

## (iii) Take a lean approach to multimedia and interactive content

Multimedia content such as photos and videos, and interactive elements such as maps, can add significantly to the 'weight' (the size in bytes) of a page.

Only use multimedia and interactive content when there is a clear user need, and allow users to make explicit choices to access multimedia and interactive content, rather than loading it by default.

## (iv) Design for lightweight and flexible notifications

Notifications, such as emails or texts, involve data transfer and energy consumption. This can increase when they have attachments, and for services with many users these impacts can quickly mount.

Take a lean approach to notifications by:

- Only sending emails, Short Message Service (SMS) texts, and web notifications when genuinely needed
- Keeping notifications as lightweight (as small a file size) as possible
- Setting up notifications so users can turn them off or choose how often to receive them according to their preference

## (v) Choose lightweight publishing formats for documents

The file format of documents impact file sizes, and the energy consumption coming from their transfer.

For example, publishing documents as well-designed HyperText Markup Language (HTML) webpages rather than Portable Document Format (PDF) can avoid unnecessary document downloads and reduce the amount of data transferred, particularly where PDFs contain large images or complex layouts.

Choose document formats with lower filesizes and more flexibility where possible.
