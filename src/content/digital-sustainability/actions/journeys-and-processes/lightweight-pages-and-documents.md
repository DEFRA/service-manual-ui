**Applies to:** anything you publish or send to users, including pages of services on and off CDP, internal tools, notifications and documents.

**Lead role families:** Design and Software

---

## (i) Set page weight budgets

A page weight budget is an upper limit on the size of a web page, measured in bytes. Larger pages mean more data transfer, and more energy use.

A page weight budget helps because it:

- makes page weight a constraint that designers, developers and content teams work to throughout delivery
- stops page weight creeping up as you add features, media and technologies

To check pages against your budget, see [test for page weight](/digital-sustainability/actions/software/efficient-testing).

<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, setting a page weight budget brings co-benefits in terms of:

- inclusion: budgets make teams think hard about what is essential for users with small screens or low bandwidth, so pages work on slow connections

## (ii) Design for text-based journeys

Design pages and journeys to work as text first. Users should be able to understand and complete every core task without images, video or other media.

Text-first journeys also support progressive enhancement. For how to build this, see [develop pages using progressive enhancement](/digital-sustainability/actions/software/energy-efficient-software).

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, designing for text-based journeys brings co-benefits in terms of:

- inclusion: pages perform better on slower connections and older devices, which extends the useful life of hardware and reduces pressure to upgrade
- accessibility: well-structured text works well with screen readers, zoom, translation and different types of device

</div>
</details>

## (iii) Take a lean approach to multimedia and interactive content

Photos, video and interactive elements such as maps can add a lot to the weight of a page, that is, its size in bytes.

Only use multimedia and interactive content when there is a clear user need. Let users choose to open it. For loading media only when it is needed, see [use lazy loading](/digital-sustainability/actions/software/energy-efficient-software).

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Taking a lean approach to multimedia brings co-benefits in terms of:

- user experience: pages load faster for everyone
- inclusion: pages with less multimedia and interactive content work better for users with slow connections

</div>
</details>

## (iv) Design lightweight and flexible notifications

Notifications, such as emails and text messages, transfer data and use energy. Attachments add to this, and for services with many users the impact soon adds up.

Keep notifications lean:

- only send emails, text messages (SMS) and web notifications when they are needed
- keep them as small as you can
- let users turn notifications off or choose how often they get them

Government services can send emails, text messages and letters through [GOV.UK Notify](https://www.notifications.service.gov.uk/).

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Designing lightweight and flexible notifications brings co-benefits in terms of:

- user experience: short, simple notifications are quicker to understand and easier to read on a mobile
- accessibility and inclusion: simple, text-based messages are more likely to work across different devices, assistive technologies and slower connections

</div>
</details>

## (v) Choose lightweight formats for documents

Publish documents as HTML pages, not PDFs. People read them without a download and you send less data. This matters most where a PDF contains large images or a complex layout.

When you choose a format, pick the one with the smallest file size and the most flexibility that still meets the need.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Choosing lightweight publishing formats brings co-benefits in terms of:

- accessibility: semantic HTML works more reliably than PDF with screen readers, keyboard navigation, text resizing and user-defined styles
- user experience: users can read content straight away in the service rather than downloading and opening a separate document

</div>
</details>
