## (i) Adopt approaches to extend hardware lifecycles

Keeping existing devices in use for longer means fewer replacements, and avoids the environmental impact of making and disposing of them.

These approaches help hardware last longer:

<table class="govuk-table"><thead class="govuk-table__head"><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th class="govuk-table__header">Approach</th><th class="govuk-table__header">Description</th></tr></thead><tbody class="govuk-table__body"><tr class="govuk-table__row"><td class="govuk-table__cell">Proactive device maintenance</td><td class="govuk-table__cell">Physical care and power protection, good battery management, and encouraging staff to look after their devices. All of these keep equipment working.</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><td class="govuk-table__cell">Redeploying hardware internally</td><td class="govuk-table__cell">A hardware return loop. Faulty or unneeded equipment is collected, repaired or refurbished, and kept as reserve stock. Devices can be redeployed within the department or across government. Older hardware can be given to less demanding tasks.</td></tr></tbody></table>

Extending life is not always the better choice. Newer hardware can be much more energy efficient. Take power-hungry equipment such as an old server. The energy saved by replacing it can outweigh the embodied carbon of a new one: the carbon from making it. Compare both before you decide.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, extending hardware lifecycles brings co-benefits in terms of:

- cost: keeping devices in use longer delays the cost of buying replacements
- resilience: a reserve stock of refurbished devices makes replacements quicker when kit fails

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- usability and performance: older devices can run slowly, which frustrates staff and lowers productivity
- what to watch: Defra Group IT buys staff devices, so service teams mostly influence this through requirements

</div>
</details>

## (ii) Follow security best practice for supporting hardware

Good security protects devices from malware, overheating and early component failure. It also helps avoid investigations and fixes that use a lot of carbon and resources.

Follow this security guidance:

- [Supply chain security guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/supply-chain-security) (National Cyber Security Centre)
- [Supply chain guidance](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/security-best-practices/supply-chain-guidance) (National Protective Security Authority)
- [Choosing, buying and deploying devices securely](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/device-security-guidance/getting-ready) (National Cyber Security Centre): follow this when you buy devices

Ask suppliers to follow [secure sanitisation and disposal of storage media](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/secure-sanitisation-storage-media) (National Cyber Security Centre). Sanitisation means removing data so it cannot be recovered.

Devices that no longer get security updates must be retired, whatever their condition. Plan their reuse or disposal.

<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, following security best practice for hardware brings co-benefits in terms of:

- security: patched, well-managed devices are less likely to be compromised
- cost: preventing incidents avoids the cost of investigation, recovery and replacement
- resilience: protecting devices from malware and overheating reduces unplanned failures

</div>
</details>

## (iii) Design new systems to work on older devices

Backward compatibility means designing new systems so that older equipment still works with them. People can keep using their devices as software changes, so fewer devices are replaced before they need to be.

Design new services to work on older devices, for external and internal users. Keep pages light and support existing file formats. See [2b. Design lightweight pages, notifications and documents](/digital-sustainability/actions/journeys-and-processes/lightweight-pages-and-documents).

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, supporting older devices brings co-benefits in terms of:

- cost: keeping older devices in use longer avoids the cost of switching to new hardware

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- legacy code: supporting old formats can mean running less efficient code, which uses more processing power and data centre energy

</div>
</details>
