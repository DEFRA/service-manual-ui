<div class="app-applies-to">

**This action applies to:** infrastructure projects, commercial teams buying hosting, and services hosted outside the Core Delivery Platform (CDP). A team on CDP can ask the CDP team what figures it can give for its service.

**Lead role families:** Procurement, Product and delivery, and Architecture

</div>

---

## (i) Choose providers that report at service level

Hosting providers can report energy use, carbon, water and heat reuse at several levels. These range from the whole facility down to single servers and software services. This lets operators track efficiency and allocate costs.

Choose providers that can report these figures for an individual service or project. This shows you where to find savings and what to improve.

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Co-benefits</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Alongside sustainability impact, service-level sustainability reporting brings co-benefits in terms of:

- cost: emissions hotspots often match cost hotspots, so one fix can cut both
- compliance: service-level figures make Greening Government Commitments reporting more accurate
- data quality: measured figures replace estimates, so teams can see whether changes worked

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Trade-offs</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

Potential trade-offs may include:

- time lag: provider figures often arrive the month after use, so they suit trends rather than live decisions
- shared platforms: on a shared platform such as CDP, shared resources must be split across services by an agreed method
- comparability: provider methods differ and change, so figures from different suppliers may not compare

</div>
</details>

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Further reading</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

- [Software Carbon Intensity specification](https://sci.greensoftware.foundation/) (Green Software Foundation): a method for calculating emissions per unit of work, such as per user or per transaction

</div>
</details>

## (ii) Choose providers that maximise server utilisation

Choose providers that help you keep servers busy. In the cloud this means right-sizing (matching resources to what you use) and autoscaling (adding or removing capacity as demand changes). On or off premises, it means consolidating workloads onto fewer servers.

See [5b. Provision cloud services efficiently](/digital-sustainability/actions/architecture/cloud-provisioning) and [5d. Maximise efficiency for on-premises workloads](/digital-sustainability/actions/architecture/on-premises-workloads).

---

## (iii) Choose providers that extend hardware life and dispose of it well

Choose providers with a plan to manage retired hardware sustainably. The plan should start at the top of the waste hierarchy, which ranks options from best to worst, with reuse before recycling.

See [8c. Choose the most sustainable options for hardware at end of life](/digital-sustainability/actions/hardware/end-of-life).
