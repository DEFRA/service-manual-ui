## Artificial Intelligence and sustainability in Defra

Defra has a position statement on how sustainability is considered with the use of AI. It can be accessed by those in the department on the 'Ethics' section of the [AI Governance](https://defra.sharepoint.com.mcas.ms/teams/Team4303/SitePages/AI-Governance.aspx/) page.

## Sustainability for AI controlled by the organisation versus AI integrated in wider software systems

With Artificial Intelligence technologies, there is a commonly a distinction is how much control the organisation has over the AI model and therefore how directly it can influence its sustainability impacts, depending on:

- Organisation-controlled AI: If an organisation develops, fine-tunes, selects or directly operates an AI model, it has greater control over decisions such as model size, training approach, hardware, hosting location, frequency of retraining and inference configuration.
- AI integrated into wider software or 'Software-as-a-Service' (SaaS): If AI is embedded within a third-party product, the organisation may have limited or no control over the underlying model, training or infrastructure

When an organisation controls AI models, there may be more opportunities to reduce energy and resource consumption directly. When AI is part of a wider software system, the supplier can still implement appropriate Digital Sustainability Actions relating to AI, and provide reporting on which actions they have been able to take.

Delivering AI that is as environmentally sustainable as possible alos requires that best practice is followed for some other aspects of digital services. See the following pages for more guidance:
