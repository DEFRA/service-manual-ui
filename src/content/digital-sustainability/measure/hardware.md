Suppliers can give a range of metrics to show the environmental and social credentials of hardware that is rented, bought, monitored and retired.

The most complete measure is a whole-lifecycle assessment. The [UNDP Sustainable Procurement of ICT Equipment Guide](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-09/sustainable_procurement_of_ict_equipment.pdf) (UNDP, PDF) and the [Hardware Lifecycle Management Toolkit](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ad7573b8ef5e8a9fc8ed5f5/Circular_Economy_Working_Group_Hardware_Lifecycle_Management_Toolkit.pdf) (Circular Economy Working Group, PDF) set out the metrics.

## New devices

### Ecolabel certifications

An ecolabel covers a wide range of environmental and social issues through a single, externally assured standard that is kept up to date.

Type I ecolabels have clearly defined, third-party assured criteria for devices, and should conform to ISO 14024. Type II ecolabels are self-declared environmental claims and must conform to ISO 14021.

You can report on:

- the number of new devices bought or rented that meet Type I ecolabels, such as EPEAT or TCO
- the number of new devices bought or rented that meet Type II ecolabels

### Sustainability impact reports and lifecycle assessments

Hardware may also have a sustainability impact report or lifecycle assessment. These can cover environmental performance and impact metrics such as:

- estimated power use, in modelled idle, active and rated scenarios
- embodied carbon: the carbon from making and transporting the device
- embodied water, in cubic metres: the water used to make the device
- proportion and weight of recycled materials
- use of critical minerals
- energy efficiency certification
- weight of packaging

<div class="govuk-inset-text">

Metrics should be calculated in line with ISO 14040 and 14044, or another recognised method.

</div>

## Second-life devices

Second-life devices have been used before, and are refurbished, remanufactured or reused. Third-party certifications can show the quality of second-life devices, such as:

- [ISO 9001](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/products-and-services/standards/iso-9001-quality-management-systems/) (quality management systems)
- [BS 8887-220:2010](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/design-for-manufacture-assembly-disassembly-and-end-of-life-processing-made-the-process-of-remanufacture-specification) (remanufactured products)
- [BS 8887-240:2011](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/design-for-manufacture-assembly-disassembly-and-end-of-life-processing-made-reconditioning) (reconditioned products)
- [PAS 141:2011](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/reuse-of-used-and-waste-electrical-and-electronic-equipment-ueee-and-weee-process-management-specification) (reuse of used and waste electrical and electronic equipment)
- [BS EN IEC 62368-1:2024+A11:2024](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/audio-video-information-and-communication-technology-equipment-safety-requirements-4) (ICT safety requirements)

## Device performance

You can monitor the performance of new and second-life devices:

- energy used by devices (kWh)
- greenhouse gas emissions from devices
- the number of devices kept beyond the standard refresh cycle
- the number of refurbished and remanufactured devices deployed

## End of life

Dispose of hardware in line with the waste hierarchy: repair, refurbish, reuse or donate devices before you recycle them.

End-of-life reporting can cover the volume of hardware:

- repaired
- refurbished
- redeployed
- reused externally, split into donated and sold
- recycled
- sent to landfill
- incinerated, split into with and without energy recovery
- the weight of components recovered for reuse, and of materials (including critical minerals) recovered for recycling

For how a service records the e-waste it produces, read [other sustainability impacts](/digital-sustainability/measure/other-impacts).

Reporting should also confirm that data has been securely sanitised or destroyed in line with security policies.
