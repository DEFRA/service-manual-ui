The ISO/IEC 30134 series is an international family of standards that defines standardized Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for measuring and reporting the operational resource efficiency and environmental impact of data centres.

</div>
</details>

Hosting providers can report at the following levels:

- Facility-level
- Service-level

---

## Facility-level metrics

Facility-level metrics describe the environmental performance of the whole data centre, with metrics relating to energy use, as well as carbon and water.

These metrics can guide decisions on which data centre providers to choose through procurement, as well as being crucial to calculate data centre overhead and convert energy use figures into estimates of carbon and water. See the pages on [measurement](/digital-sustainability/measure) for more information.

<div class="govuk-inset-text">

A data centre can have a great PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness), meaning it wastes little energy on cooling and lighting, yet still produce a heavy carbon footprint if it draws power from a fossil-fuel-heavy grid. Carbon usage effectiveness (CUE) shifts the focus from efficiency to decarbonisation.

</div>

### Reporting on resilience

Reporting on the resilience of the data centre services and subcontractors can focus on climate change, severe weather events (such as flooding and extreme temperatures) and ecological degradation, including:

- Number of resilience risks identified affecting service continuity, infrastructure, supply chains and critical dependencies
- Actions taken to improve resilience, adaptation and continuity plans

## Service-level metrics

Service-level metrics describe the share of environmental impact attributable to a particular cloud or hosting service.
