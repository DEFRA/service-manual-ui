Hosting providers can report at 2 levels:

- the facility
- the service

<details class="govuk-details">
<summary class="govuk-details__summary"><span class="govuk-details__summary-text">Read more about the ISO standard for data centres</span></summary>
<div class="govuk-details__text">

The ISO/IEC 30134 series is a family of international standards that defines standard key performance indicators (KPIs) for measuring and reporting the resource efficiency and environmental impact of data centres.

</div>
</details>

## Facility-level metrics

Facility-level metrics describe the environmental performance of the whole data centre: its energy use, carbon and water.

Use them to choose data centre providers when you buy hosting. You also need them to add data centre overhead and to convert energy figures into carbon and water. Read about [adding data centre overhead](/digital-sustainability/measure/data-centre-carbon), [converting energy to carbon](/digital-sustainability/measure/carbon) and [water](/digital-sustainability/measure/water).

<div class="govuk-inset-text">

A data centre can have a good power usage effectiveness (PUE), wasting little energy on cooling and lighting, and still have a heavy carbon footprint if its electricity comes from a fossil-fuel-heavy grid. Carbon usage effectiveness (CUE) shifts the focus from efficiency to decarbonisation.

</div>

It is standardised in ISO/IEC 30134-2.</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Renewable energy factor (REF)</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">The ratio of renewable energy a data centre uses to its total energy use. Where possible, separate renewable electricity generated on site from electricity bought through power purchase agreements.</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Cooling efficiency ratio (CER)</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">The ratio of a cooling system's cooling output to its electrical input</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Energy reuse factor (ERF)</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">The ratio of reused waste energy, such as server heat, exported outside the facility to the total energy used</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Carbon usage effectiveness (CUE)</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">The total carbon emissions of a data centre compared with the energy its IT equipment uses</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Water usage effectiveness (WUE)</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">The total water used for facility operations, such as cooling and humidification, divided by the total energy used by IT equipment</td></tr></tbody></table>

### Reporting on resilience

Reporting on the resilience of data centre services and subcontractors can focus on climate change, severe weather such as flooding and extreme temperatures, and ecological degradation, including:

- the number of resilience risks identified that affect service continuity, infrastructure, supply chains and critical dependencies
- actions taken to improve resilience, adaptation and continuity plans

## Service-level metrics

Service-level metrics describe the share of environmental impact that belongs to a particular cloud or hosting service.

If market-based figures are also given, report them separately.</td></tr><tr class="govuk-table__row"><th scope="row" class="govuk-table__header">Total water withdrawal and consumption</th><td class="govuk-table__cell">Absolute water withdrawal and consumption.
