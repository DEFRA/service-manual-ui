## The problem with reviewing expenses

Finance already uses controls to identify travel and expense transactions that may not comply with policy. A small team then reviews those cases one by one.

That process depends heavily on manual work or basic automation.

The team currently reviews only about 5% of the available data, focusing on repeat offenders and only parts of policy. This limits its view of potential non-compliance and makes it harder to spot repeated behaviour across weeks or months.

We wanted to test a different approach. Could technology assess the full dataset and help Finance specialists focus their time on the findings that matter most?

## More findings are not enough

Reviewing more data could create many more findings. Simply detecting more potential issues would move the problem rather than solve it.

So the proof of concept looked at the whole compliance process. That included how Finance could prioritise findings, investigate them efficiently, resolve cases at scale and monitor patterns over time.

## Giving each task to what does it best

We combined rules, data analysis and AI-assisted assessment, with people making the final decisions.

Code can check clear conditions, such as spending thresholds or missing information. Data analysis can group related transactions and show cumulative spend.

AI can help where the assessment depends on policy wording, free-text explanations and the context of a transaction.

Human judgement stays central.

The aim is not for AI to decide whether an expense complies with policy. It is to give Finance teams clearer evidence, explanations and priorities.

## Looking at patterns, not just transactions

One of the most useful changes was to organise information around an employee rather than a single transaction.

One claim may present little risk on its own. Repeated behaviour over several weeks or months, or multiple transactions grouped together, can tell a different story.

Finance operators valued being able to see those longer-term patterns.

The prototype also showed how reporting could help the team focus on the areas with the greatest risk or value, find repeated non-compliance and monitor trends across employees, expense categories and organisations.

## What we learned

Testing showed where AI might add value and where stronger foundations are needed.

AI could interpret context that simple rules would miss.

For example, it could distinguish between a justification saying alcohol was claimed and one explaining that alcohol was not claimed. It could also consider an £8 lunch in context with other transactions of the same claim, instead of treating the amount alone as the answer.

But policy must first be turned into clear, validated and testable rules. Expense types must map to the right rules, so each transaction is assessed against the correct criteria.

Better supporting data would also improve the assessment. Receipt information, clearer employee explanations and links to other Defra systems could provide a richer evidence base.

## Helping people get it right

This new capability could support efficiencies in prioritising the most important investigations, as well as improvement in detection by expanding the rules that are used.

Standard or bulk messages could ask employees for better evidence and explain what the expense policy requires. Over time, that feedback could improve the information submitted at source and make later checks more effective.

## What comes next

Finance colleagues responded positively to the ability to prioritise findings, understand patterns of behaviour and see the reasoning behind an assessment.

The next step is to decide how this capability could fit into Finance ways of working. That includes which checks should use rules, where AI could help, what additional data is appropriate and how the team would manage a larger volume of findings.

The approach could also be useful across Defra's arm's-length bodies. Any extension would need to account for different policies, datasets and transaction volumes.

This proof of concept did not replace a compliance process or make final decisions. It showed how AI can inform human expertise and help them focus on the most valuable work to give Finance a broader and more useful view of expense compliance.

This was a proof of concept, not a live service. Before it could become one, it would need more work on policy, data and security. It would also need testing with the people who would use it day to day.
